# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be civil. Assume the other person is acting in good faith. Critique the code, the design or the
decision — not the person who made it.

## What is expected

- **Disagree about technical substance.** "This allocation is unbounded" is useful. "You clearly
  don't understand allocations" is not, and says the same thing less well.
- **Say what you actually observed.** "It panics on this file, here is the file" moves a bug toward
  fixed. "It's broken" does not.
- **Accept that being wrong is normal.** This project's own changelog contains several entries
  where earlier work was measured, found wanting, and reverted. That is the process working, not a
  failure.
- **Respect that people contribute in different amounts and at different levels.** A one-line typo
  fix and a parser rewrite get the same courtesy.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks of any kind.
- Discriminatory language or imagery relating to race, ethnicity, nationality, gender, gender
  identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, religion, or experience level.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussion — repeatedly reopening a settled decision, or arguing in bad
  faith.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, commit messages, code comments, and any space where someone
is participating in this project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to the maintainer through the repository's issue tracker, or privately using the
contact in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) if the matter is sensitive. Reports are read, taken seriously,
and handled without involving the reporter publicly.

Responses scale with the problem: a private word for a first minor lapse, a request to stop, removal
of the offending content, and — for a sustained pattern — a ban from the repository. The maintainer
decides, and will explain the reasoning.
